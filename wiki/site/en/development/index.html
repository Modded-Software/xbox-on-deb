Development

StarCraft: Ghost passed through three studios in four years. Blizzard owned the brand, Nihilistic Software built a stealth-action game, and Swingin' Ape Studios rebuilt it around multiplayer. An identity crisis, staff churn, and the success of World of Warcraft left the game on indefinite hold.

The original pitch

Blizzard wanted a console entry in the StarCraft series and turned to Nihilistic, a studio founded in 1999 by former LucasArts developers. Nihilistic's Ray Gresko had worked on Jedi Knight, and the studio pitched a third-person game that leaned on stealth, acrobatics, and a small set of military gadgets. Blizzard's early plan had the player as a male marine; the team shifted to a Ghost, then to Nova Terra as the single protagonist.

The identity crisis

Polygon's 2016 postmortem, built from interviews with nine developers, describes a project pulled between two designs. Nihilistic wanted a squad-based game with class roles, which the studio called Strike Teams. Blizzard wanted one hero and a tighter stealth-action loop. The game was rebuilt more than once, and each rebuild reset production. Nihilistic also chafed under Blizzard's milestone reviews.

At E3 2004, Blizzard's leadership told Nihilistic the relationship was over. The split was announced in June 2004, and development moved to Swingin' Ape Studios, a studio known for the Nintendo GameCube game Metal Arms: Glitch in the System.

The Swingin' Ape era

Swingin' Ape rebuilt the game around competitive multiplayer, with playable classes and modes such as Deathmatch, Capture the Flag, and the StarCraft-flavoured Capture the Base and Invasion. Blizzard acquired the studio in May 2005. That November, the GameCube version was cancelled, leaving the PlayStation 2 and Xbox.

The multiplayer work is not in the leaked build. The May 8 2004 artifact predates Swingin' Ape's tenure, so it shows the Nihilistic single-player foundation and none of the later multiplayer.

The hold and cancellation

In March 2006, Blizzard announced it was refocusing its console team and placing StarCraft: Ghost on indefinite hold. At the 2011 D.I.C.E. Summit, Blizzard CEO Mike Morhaime said the runaway success of World of Warcraft pulled the staff and resources the game needed. Blizzard confirmed the cancellation in 2014.
